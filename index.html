Official Guidelines for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Document Index

Administrative Directive

Prior to initiation, all review protocols must be registered prospectively (e.g., PROSPERO) and approved by the institutional ethical committee.

Section I

Scope & Length Expectations

The Faculty of Medicine at Tanta University evaluates postgraduate theses on the basis of methodological rigor, scientific contribution, and clarity of synthesis rather than strict word count parameters. However, normative length frameworks exist for administrative and review purposes.

The Meta-Analysis Exception

Unlike primary clinical trials requiring extensive patient data logging, systematic reviews and meta-analyses are inherently concise documents. Evaluators prioritize strict adherence to PRISMA guidelines and robust statistical synthesis. A highly focused document of 70-90 pages is deemed superior to artificially inflated texts.

Institutional Page Norms

Degree Program Standard Thesis Meta-Analysis (Expected)
Master's (MSc) 80 - 150 pages 60 - 90 pages
Doctorate (MD) 150 - 250+ pages 80 - 120 pages

*Page counts exclude preliminary pages, reference lists, and extensive supplementary appendices.

Target Word Distribution (Excluding References)

  • I. Introduction & Background 2,000 - 3,500 words
  • II. Methodology 3,000 - 5,000 words
  • III. Results 2,500 - 4,000 words
  • IV. Discussion & Conclusions 3,000 - 4,500 words
  • Total Main Body Estimation ~10,500 - 17,000 words

Section II

Thesis Architecture

The structural integrity of a meta-analysis differs fundamentally from traditional empirical research. Proportional emphasis must be placed on methodology and results processing.

Proportional Content Distribution

1. Introduction / Review of Literature

Requires brevity. The literature review must culminate in a clear justification for the meta-analysis, explicitly highlighting conflicting primary trials, knowledge gaps, or the necessity for consensus.

2. Methodology (Core Component)

Must rigorously define the PICO criteria, exact search syntax for all utilized databases (e.g., PubMed, Cochrane, Scopus), explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria, dual-reviewer selection processes, Risk of Bias (RoB) tools utilized, and specific statistical models (fixed vs. random effects, heterogeneity assessment metrics like $I^2$).

3. Results

Must feature the PRISMA Flow Diagram detailing study attrition. Requires comprehensive tables defining the baseline characteristics of included studies, followed by forest plots for primary and secondary outcomes, and funnel plots assessing publication bias.

4. Discussion

Contextualization of the synthesized effect sizes against current clinical guidelines. A mandatory section detailing the limitations of the included primary studies and the meta-analysis itself is required.

Section IV

Mandatory Formatting Regulations

A. Typographical Standards

  • Primary Typeface (English): Times New Roman, 14 pt.
  • Primary Typeface (Arabic Summary): Simplified Arabic, 14 pt.
  • Headings: 16 pt Bold (Chapter Titles), 14 pt Bold (Subheadings).
  • Line Spacing: Exactly 1.5 lines throughout the main body.
  • Margin Constraints: 3.5 cm on the binding edge (Left for English text), 2.5 cm on all other margins (Top, Bottom, Right).
  • Pagination: Centered at the bottom. Roman numerals (I, II, III) for preliminary pages; Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3) commencing from the Introduction.

B. Citation Protocol

The Faculty of Medicine strictly enforces the Vancouver Citation Style. Citations must appear as bracketed or superscript Arabic numerals in the text, ordered consecutively as they appear.

// Standard Journal Article Format [1] Author AB, Author CD, Author EF. Title of the peer-reviewed article. Abbreviated Journal Name. Year of Publication;Volume(Issue):StartPage-EndPage.
