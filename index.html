Section I

Scope & Length Expectations

This section details the normative page and word counts expected by the Faculty of Medicine. It clarifies the common misconception that longer theses equate to higher quality, establishing the baseline parameters for a concise, high-impact meta-analysis document.

The Faculty of Medicine at Tanta University evaluates postgraduate theses on the basis of methodological rigor, scientific contribution, and clarity of synthesis rather than strict word count parameters. However, normative length frameworks exist for administrative and review purposes.

The Meta-Analysis Exception

Unlike primary clinical trials requiring extensive patient data logging, systematic reviews and meta-analyses are inherently concise documents. Evaluators prioritize strict adherence to PRISMA 2020 guidelines and robust statistical synthesis. A highly focused document of 70-90 pages is deemed superior to artificially inflated texts.

Institutional Page Norms

Degree Program Standard Thesis Meta-Analysis (Expected)
Master's (MSc) 80 - 150 pages 60 - 90 pages
Doctorate (MD) 150 - 250+ pages 80 - 120 pages

*Page counts exclude preliminary pages, reference lists, and extensive supplementary appendices.

Target Word Distribution (Excluding References)

  • I. Introduction & Background 2,000 - 3,500 words
  • II. Methodology 3,000 - 5,000 words
  • III. Results 2,500 - 4,000 words
  • IV. Discussion & Conclusions 3,000 - 4,500 words
  • Total Main Body Estimation ~10,500 - 17,000 words

Section II

Thesis Architecture

This section visualizes the precise breakdown of chapters required for a systematic review. You will explore an interactive breakdown of content allocation, demonstrating how a meta-analysis fundamentally shifts focus away from the introduction and heavily toward the methodology and results synthesis.

Proportional Emphasis

Unlike narrative reviews, the structural integrity of a meta-analysis relies heavily on transparent, reproducible methods. The chart illustrates the recommended allocation of effort and space within the final document.

Key Insight:

Methodology and Results combined should constitute approximately 60% of your entire thesis volume.

1. Introduction

Requires brevity. The literature review must culminate in a clear justification for the meta-analysis, explicitly highlighting conflicting primary trials, knowledge gaps, or the necessity for clinical consensus.

2. Methodology

Must rigorously define the PICO criteria, exact search syntax for all utilized databases, explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria, dual-reviewer selection processes, Risk of Bias tools, and specific statistical models.

3. Results

Must feature the PRISMA Flow Diagram detailing study attrition. Requires comprehensive tables defining the baseline characteristics of included studies, forest plots, and funnel plots assessing bias.

4. Discussion

Contextualization of the synthesized effect sizes against current clinical guidelines. A mandatory section detailing the limitations of the included primary studies and the meta-analysis itself is required.

Section IV

Mandatory Formatting Regulations

This section serves as a strict visual and typographical reference guide. Here, you will find the exact margin, font, and pagination constraints required by the postgraduate affairs office, ensuring your manuscript is not rejected for stylistic non-compliance.

■ A. Typographical Standards

  • Primary Font (English): Times New Roman, 14 pt.
  • Primary Font (Arabic): Simplified Arabic, 14 pt.
  • Headings: 16 pt Bold (Chapter Titles), 14 pt Bold (Subheadings).
  • Line Spacing: Exactly 1.5 lines throughout the main body.
  • Margin Constraints: 3.5 cm on the binding edge (Left for English), 2.5 cm on Top, Bottom, Right.
  • Pagination: Bottom center. Roman numerals (I, II, III) for preliminary pages; Arabic numerals (1, 2) commencing from Introduction.

■ B. Citation Protocol

The Faculty of Medicine strictly enforces the Vancouver Citation Style. Citations must appear as bracketed or superscript Arabic numerals in the text, ordered consecutively as they appear. See the official NLM / ICMJE Vancouver reference guide and Citing Medicine (NCBI).

Standard Journal Article Format Example [1] Author AB, Author CD. Title of the peer-reviewed article. Abbreviated Journal Name. Year of Publication;Volume(Issue):StartPage-EndPage.

Section V

TUREC Clearance & Admin Roadmap

This section outlines the bureaucratic progression specific to Tanta University. It provides a linear timeline of administrative steps, detailing how to secure the necessary ethical approvals and departmental sign-offs prior to conducting your data extraction.

Step 1

Departmental Protocol Seminar (حلقة البحث)

Before formally submitting any paperwork, you must present your research concept to your specific department council (e.g., Orthopedic Surgery). You must prepare a concise presentation defining the clinical gap, your precise PICO question, and the planned search strategy databases. Approval here is required to proceed to ethical review.

Step 2

TUREC Ethical Clearance

Even though systematic reviews analyze secondary data and do not involve direct patient contact, the Tanta University Research Ethics Committee (TUREC) still mandates an official review to grant an Exemption Certificate.

Required Submissions:

  • Full protocol document signed by all supervisors.
  • Formal Arabic summary of the protocol.
  • Completed TUREC application forms.

Step 3

PROSPERO & Postgraduate Registration

Upon receiving TUREC clearance, register the protocol title internationally via the PROSPERO database to prevent duplication of effort. Review the PROSPERO registration guidance first. Concurrently, submit the final, signed dossier to the Vice Dean for Postgraduate Affairs at the Faculty of Medicine to formally lock the thesis title under your name.

Section VI

Modern Digital Workflow Toolkit

This section transitions from rules to active execution. It provides actionable frameworks for utilizing digital tools, structured data matrices, and advanced biostatistics software to dramatically increase the efficiency and accuracy of your screening and analysis phases.

1. Boolean Search Strategy Construction

Translate your PICO criteria into robust database queries using proper syntax. Use the MeSH Browser to find controlled vocabulary and the Polyglot Search Translator to convert a PubMed query for Scopus, Embase, etc.

Example Clinical Query (Orthopedics Trauma) ("Tibia Fractures"[MeSH Terms] OR "Distal Tibia Fracture"[Title/Abstract] OR "Fibula Fracture"[Title/Abstract])
AND ("Arthroscopy"[MeSH Terms] OR "Arthroscopic Repair"[Title/Abstract])
AND ("Open Fracture Reduction"[MeSH Terms] OR "ORIF"[Title/Abstract])

2. Blinded Screening via Rayyan

Abandon Excel for the initial screening phase. Export your bulk search results from PubMed/Cochrane as .RIS files and import them into the Rayyan web platform. It automatically detects duplicates and allows you and your co-reviewer to independently vote "Include" or "Exclude" blindly, satisfying PRISMA blinding requirements instantly. (Covidence is a strong Cochrane-endorsed alternative.)

3. Reference Management

Never format citations manually. Utilize software such as Mendeley, Zotero (free & open-source), or EndNote. Install the Microsoft Word plugin, import your final included studies, and select the Vancouver style. The software will auto-generate and update your bibliography dynamically as you write.

4. Standardized Data Extraction Matrix

When moving to full-text review, extract data directly into a pre-formatted spreadsheet utilizing these essential column headers to prevent redundant reading sessions:

Study ID (Author, Year) Country Study Design Total N Mean Age / Demographics Intervention Specifics Control Details Primary Outcome Data (Mean ± SD / Events) Follow-up Duration

5. Risk of Bias (RoB) Tool Matching

Applying the incorrect quality assessment tool will result in thesis revision. Match the tool to the primary study design:

  • RCTs → Cochrane RoB 2.0
  • Observational → Newcastle-Ottawa Scale
  • Non-RCT Trials → ROBINS-I

Tip: visualise your judgements with the free robvis traffic-light plot tool.

6. Advanced Biostatistics Setup

For data synthesis and forest plot generation, transition away from basic tools and utilize the R programming language.

Installing R Studio and utilizing dedicated statistical packages such as meta and metafor provides superior control over random-effects modeling and generates highly professional, publication-ready data plots. No coding? Try RevMan or the free Excel-based Meta-Essentials.

■ Frequently Asked Questions

Does a systematic review / meta-analysis need ethical approval?

It analyses already-published data, but Tanta University (TUREC) still requires an official review to issue an exemption certificate. I help you prepare and submit that paperwork correctly.

How long does a meta-analysis usually take?

Typically 3–6 months depending on the number of studies, your availability, and whether the data needs extensive extraction. I give you a realistic timeline up front.

Do I need to know R or statistics?

No. I can run the full analysis for you and explain every output so you can confidently defend it — or teach you to do it yourself if you prefer.

Can you help if I've already started or collected data?

Absolutely. I can join at any stage — review your protocol, rescue a stalled search, or take your extracted data straight to analysis and write-up.

Do you work remotely and with non-Tanta students?

Yes. Mentoring is fully remote via WhatsApp, email, and video calls, so I work with researchers at any institution. The handbook here is tailored to Tanta, but the method is universal.
